Synthesia review for small business

Synthesia turns a script, a slide deck, or a PDF into a video with an AI presenter reading it, so you never get on camera. It's free to try with a watermark. Paid plans start at $19 a month, and every video spends credits.

#4Our rank · see the six-rank keyIt's alright

“Training videos without getting on camera.” Useful in the right setup. Don't rearrange your life for it. Researched

WHY YOU SHOULD CARE

If you need training, onboarding, or how-to videos and hate being on camera, Synthesia gives you a presenter, a voice, and captions without a mic or a film crew. Synthesia says it can also translate a finished video into 140+ languages.

If you don't make videos to teach or explain something, skip it.

WHEN TO USE IT

  • You make training, onboarding, or how-to videos and hate being on camera.
  • You need the same video in more than one language.
  • You update your training often and want to edit text instead of reshooting.
  • You already have scripts or slide decks sitting around.

WHEN TO SKIP IT

  • You make one or two videos a year. Use the free plan or your phone.
  • Your customers buy from you because they know your face. A real you beats an avatar.
  • You need social clips and ads more than training. Look at CapCut or Canva first.
  • You don't want another monthly subscription that runs on credits.

HOW TO USE IT

Step 1: Sign up for the free Basic plan at synthesia.io. Synthesia's features page says no credit card is required.

Step 2: Start a new video from a template, paste your script, and pick a stock avatar and voice [CHECK: exact menu names].

Step 3: Use this script for a first test, then generate and watch the result:

Hi, I'm here to walk you through [your process] in under two minutes. Step one: [first step]. Step two: [second step]. Step three: [third step]. If you get stuck, call us at [your number].

WHAT YOU'LL GET

A short video with a stock AI presenter reading your script, and a Synthesia watermark if you're on the free plan. Expect to fix pronunciation of names and local terms, and judge for yourself whether the presenter looks natural enough for your customers.

We have not run our own tests for this review.

WHAT YOU'RE MISSING

Here's how the plans line up, per Synthesia's pricing page on Nov 26, 2026.

ProSeveral credit levels [CHECK]Up to about 720 min of Studio video in credits, 180+ stock avatars
Team$99/month listedUp to 5 editors, shared credit pool

The catches:

  • Credits are one shared pool for video, dubbing, surveys, and AI b-roll. Synthesia says a typical finished minute of avatar video uses about 100 credits.
  • Credits reset every billing period. Extra credits you buy are good for 12 months.
  • Lip-synced dubbing costs 80 credits a minute, or 40 without lip-sync.
  • A Studio Express-1 avatar is $1,000 a year, annual plans only, and can take up to 10 days to process.

Bottom line: Start on the free plan. Pay only once you know you'll make videos every month.

PRIVACY

Synthesia says it applies content moderation to all videos. Making a personal avatar means recording your own face and voice.

We did not read Synthesia's data and likeness terms for this review [CHECK: how your face, voice, and scripts are stored and used]. Read them before you create an avatar of yourself or an employee.

ALTERNATIVES

  • HeyGen: Another AI avatar video tool [CHECK: not reviewed here yet].
  • CapCut: If you'll film yourself and just need captions and quick edits. Read our CapCut review.
  • Loom: If a quick screen recording with your real voice does the job. Read our Loom review.

Price

Free: Basic, $0: 500 credits a month, 10 minutes of Studio video or dubbing, videos carry a Synthesia watermark. No credit card required, per Synthesia.

Paid: Starter: $19 a month ($9 the first month, per the pricing page), up to about 150 minutes of Studio video in credits. Pro comes in several credit levels [CHECK: Pro price]. Team listed at $99 a month for up to 5 editors, with a $79 price shown during a promotion when checked Nov 26, 2026 [CHECK].

Does one job well: training and how-to videos without filming yourself. Most small businesses don't make enough of those to justify a subscription, which keeps it at "It's alright."
