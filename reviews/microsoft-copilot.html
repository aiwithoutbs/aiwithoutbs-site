Microsoft Copilot review for small business

Microsoft's free AI that works best if your work already lives in Word, Excel, and Outlook. Here's the deal: use the free version for quick drafts, but it only gets useful inside the apps once you pay.

Researched Verdict: Worth a try.

WHY YOU SHOULD CARE

If your business already runs on Microsoft 365, this one can draft and summarize right where you already work. Free to start. Getting it inside Word, Excel, and Outlook costs money. It still talks like it knows more than it does. You still make the final call.

WHEN TO USE IT

  • Draft an email in Outlook without starting from a blank page (paid Microsoft 365).
  • Summarize a long Word document or email thread.
  • Clean up a spreadsheet or turn numbers into a short summary.
  • Make a first draft of a PowerPoint from notes.
  • Answer everyday questions at copilot.microsoft.com, free.
  • Rewrite a messy client message into something clearer.

WHEN TO SKIP IT

  • Long, careful document work. Claude is often better there.
  • You don't use Microsoft tools and just need a basic writing bot.
  • Heavy daily use if you refuse to pay.

HOW TO USE IT

1 Go to copilot.microsoft.com and sign in with a Microsoft account.

2 Paste this prompt: "Turn these rough notes into a clean client update. Keep it short, plain, and focused on what got done and what's next: [paste notes]."

3 Read it, change anything that doesn't sound like you, then send.

WHAT YOU'LL GET

A usable draft or short summary in under a minute. It's more useful inside Word, Excel, or Outlook, which takes a paid plan. Check names, prices, and facts.

WHAT YOU'RE MISSING

Free Copilot has usage limits "subject to available capacity." Microsoft publishes no number. It also doesn't work inside Word, Excel, or Outlook. Personal: Microsoft 365 Personal $9.99/month ($99.99/year) puts Copilot in Word, Excel, PowerPoint, and Outlook. Family $12.99/month (AI for the account owner only). Premium $19.99/month. Business: Copilot add-on from $18/user/month, billed yearly, on top of your Microsoft 365 plan. Not the strongest pick for long, careful documents. Still not a lawyer or accountant. Double-check anything that costs you money or binds you.

PRIVACY

Chats can be used to improve the models unless you adjust settings; don't dump sensitive client or financial details you wouldn't want floating around.

ALTERNATIVES

Claude: better when the document is long and the wording has to sound like a grown adult wrote it. Gemini: better if your files and email already live in Google instead of Microsoft.

Price

Free: Free: $0 at copilot.microsoft.com, Edge, and the Copilot apps. Limited usage, subject to capacity (no number published).

Paid: Personal $9.99/month ($99.99/year); Family $12.99/month; Premium $19.99/month ($199.99/year); Pro $99.99/month. Business: Copilot add-on from $18/user/month (annual); Business Standard with Copilot $23.50; Business Premium with Copilot $32.

Sources (official, checked Nov 4, 2026):

  • https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/copilot/pricing/individuals
  • https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/copilot/pricing

Official site: https://copilot.microsoft.com

VERDICT

Worth a try.

Open this one if your business already runs on Microsoft. Just don't expect the free version to do much once you need it working inside Word, Excel, or Outlook.
