Loom review for small business

Loom records your screen and your face at the same time, then gives you a link to send. Explain something once on video instead of typing a 600-word email. The free plan caps you at 25 videos of 5 minutes each. The AI pieces start at $24 a user a month.

#3Our rank · see the six-rank keyBadass

“Show it once on video instead of typing it out.” Does one job really well. Point it at that job. Researched

WHY YOU SHOULD CARE

Some things take three paragraphs to type and 90 seconds to show. Loom lets you click record, walk someone through your screen while you talk, and send a link. They watch it when they have time, so nobody has to book a meeting.

If you rarely explain things to customers, staff, or contractors, skip it.

WHEN TO USE IT

  • You explain the same process to staff or contractors more than once.
  • Your customers ask how-to questions that are easier to show than type.
  • You work with people in other time zones or on other schedules.
  • You want to send a personal video to leads.

WHEN TO SKIP IT

  • You'd rather pick up the phone, and so would your customers.
  • You only need to record a video a few times a year. The free plan covers that.
  • You only want AI meeting notes. A dedicated notetaker may fit better.
  • Your computer already has a screen recorder you're happy with [CHECK: Windows and Mac include basic ones].

HOW TO USE IT

Step 1: Sign up free at loom.com and install the desktop app or the Chrome extension [CHECK: install options not on the pages we checked].

Step 2: Pick "Screen and camera," open the thing you want to explain, and hit record. Keep it under 5 minutes; that is the free plan's limit.

Step 3: Stop recording, copy the link, and paste it into your email or text. Loom gives each video its own page with a transcript and a comment box.

WHAT YOU'LL GET

A video link anyone can open, with a written transcript and closed captions, on the free plan. Pay for Business + AI and Loom writes the title, summary, and chapters for you and cuts out the "ums" and silences.

Loom publishes its own numbers (for example, "18% more viewer engagement" with the AI). Those are Loom's claims, not ours. We have not run our own tests for this review.

WHAT YOU'RE MISSING

Here's how the money works.

The catches:

  • Yearly billing saves 17%, per Loom. You pay a year up front.
  • Loom is owned by Atlassian. Buying on Atlassian.com instead of Loom.com changes how yearly plans are billed, per Loom.
  • Free videos max out at 5 minutes each. Plan your walkthrough to fit.

Bottom line: Start free. Upgrade to Business the week you hit the 25-video or 5-minute limit. Only pay the extra $6 a month for AI if you'll use the summaries and notes every week.

PRIVACY

Every plan has video privacy controls. Business adds password-protected videos and a default privacy setting for your whole workspace. Enterprise adds SSO (single sign-on, one company login for everything) and custom data retention.

Loom says its AI sends transcript text, not video or audio, to OpenAI. We did not read Loom's full AI Terms for this review [CHECK]. Read them before you record client calls.

ALTERNATIVES

  • Otter.ai — If all you want is AI notes from meetings, not screen videos. Read our Otter.ai review.
  • Descript — If you want to edit your recordings by editing the transcript, like a Word doc [CHECK: not reviewed here].
  • Your computer's built-in screen recorder — Free, no account, but no share link or transcript [CHECK].

Price

Free: Starter plan, $0: up to 25 videos per person, 5 minutes per video, 720p, up to 50 members, unlimited screenshots, transcripts in 50+ languages, comments and emoji reactions. Business + AI has a 14-day free trial, per Loom.

Paid: Per user per month: Business $18 monthly or $180 a year ($15 a month). Business + AI $24 monthly or $240 a year ($20 a month). Enterprise: contact sales, no public price. Loom says yearly billing saves 17%. Checked Nov 22, 2026.
