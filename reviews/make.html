Make.com review for small business

Make is Zapier's cheaper, more hands-on cousin. You drag boxes onto a screen, connect your apps, and the work moves itself. Now it has AI agents that can make simple judgment calls. Powerful, yes. Plug-and-play, no.

#4Our rank · see the six-rank keyIt's alright

“Cheaper automation, if you put in the time.” Useful in the right setup. Don't rearrange your life for it. Researched

WHY YOU SHOULD CARE

If you or somebody on your payroll keeps moving the same information from one app to another, Make can do that for you on a schedule.

It connects to thousands of apps, according to Make, and you build each workflow on a visual map instead of writing code.

The new AI agents are for messy jobs, like sorting incoming emails or messages, where a plain if-this-then-that rule won't cut it.

If you don't use more than two or three business apps, skip it.

WHEN TO USE IT

  • Your team re-types the same info into two or more apps every week.
  • You want lots of automations and care about the monthly bill.
  • You have messy inputs, like emails or messages, that need sorting before a person handles them. That's what Make's AI agents are for.
  • You or someone on your team doesn't mind learning a new tool.

WHEN TO SKIP IT

  • You use one or two apps and nothing needs to move between them.
  • You want the easiest setup possible. Zapier is simpler for beginners.
  • You want AI to write, research, or answer questions. Use ChatGPT or Claude for that.

HOW TO USE IT

Step 1: Sign up for the free plan at make.com.

Step 2: Create a new scenario (that's Make's word for a workflow). Pick your trigger app, like Google Forms, then the app it should send data to, like Google Sheets.

Step 3: Run it once with a test entry and check the result before you turn it on.

Ready-to-paste prompt:

I'm a small business owner using Make.com for the first time. Walk me step by step through building a scenario that takes each new Google Form response, adds it as a row in my Google Sheet, and emails me a short notice. Tell me which modules to add, what to connect, and how many credits each run will use.

WHAT YOU'LL GET

A workflow that moves information between your apps without you babysitting it.

Expect a learning curve. The visual map is clear once you get it, but the first build takes longer than it looks, and you need to test before trusting it.

We have not run our own test build for this review.

WHAT YOU'RE MISSING

Here's how the money works.

Make bills in credits. Most actions in a workflow use 1 credit. Some AI features use more. A workflow that reads a form, changes the data, and writes a row uses about three credits per run.

The catches:

  • Out of credits means your workflows stop until you buy more or upgrade.
  • Credits expire at the end of the term.
  • Make offers a Start-up Plan at 50% off Business for companies under 20 employees, if you qualify.
  • Make's pricing page loads its prices with a script, and outside reports from Oct and Nov 2026 disagree on the numbers. Confirm on make.com/pricing before you buy.

Bottom line: Start free. Pay only when one workflow is clearly saving you time.

PRIVACY

Make moves your business data between the apps you connect, so it can see whatever you give it access to.

Connect only the apps a workflow needs. Keep customer financial info and sensitive records out until you've read Make's security and privacy pages.

This review doesn't cover Make's data retention or AI training policies. Check Make's current privacy documentation before connecting sensitive information.

ALTERNATIVES

  • Zapier — Easier to learn, usually more expensive at the same volume.
  • n8n — More control for people who are technical or have a tech person.

Price

Free: $0. Up to 1,000 credits a month (Make's own FAQ). Most actions use 1 credit; some AI features use more.

Paid: Core, Pro and Teams start with 10,000 credits a month. Third-party checks in Oct and Nov 2026 list Core between $9 and $12 a month billed yearly [CHECK make.com/pricing]. Yearly billing is cheaper than monthly, per Make.

VERDICT

It's alright Rank 4 of 6: Useful in the right setup. Don't rearrange your life for it.

Make is good, cheap automation if you're willing to learn its visual builder, or you have someone who will.

If you want something you can figure out in 10 minutes, Zapier is easier. If you're going to run a lot of automations and watch the bill, Make gives you more for the money.

Start with one boring task. Automate it. See if it's worth the learning.
